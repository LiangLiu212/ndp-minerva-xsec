# VBLL event-level surrogate for the 1mu1p folded comparison (GiBUU vs MINERvA ME FHC): run writeup

This run asks whether the per-grid migration matrices of the platform's binned detector responses can be replaced by one event-level smearing of the true muon and leading-proton 4-vectors, learned by a variational Bayesian last-layer (VBLL) network, without changing the efficiency and background those responses provide. The candidate model is the heteroscedastic checkpoint `best_model_x60_het.pt` of the VBLL_SurrogateModel repository (branch sdey2_dev, commit 77c4764), trained by a collaborator on a CSV of selected events; the data are the MINERvA Medium-Energy FHC open-data playlists 1A to 1P (1.0574e21 protons on target, 329,653 selected candidates) and the model under test is the GiBUU 2025 energy-scan sample already compared with those data on 2026-09-16. Four of the plan's eleven steps were performed: the checkpoint was ported into the platform and certified bit-for-bit against the reference implementation (Step 1), the provenance of its training pairs was established by matching every training event to the platform's Playlist 1D tables (Step 2), a `vbll_event` surrogate kind was implemented and tested (Step 3), and the first grid, reconstructed muon momentum, was closed on the official MC and compared with data (Step 4). On that grid the VBLL migration conserves the predicted total and the data-to-prediction ratio (0.874 with either migration) but distorts the shape: the closure on the official MC is within 5 percent in four of eight bins and deviates by +28 percent at 7.5 to 10 GeV/c and by -30 percent at 14 to 20 GeV/c, and the Poisson -2lnL over 8 bins of the GiBUU comparison rises from 7677 to 8052. A report figure shows the GiBUU muon momentum and angle at three stages: the full charged-current sample, the truth signal, and the signal after the VBLL smearing.

## Step 1: VBLL environment and checkpoint port

The checkpoint stores only network weights, so a port needs the architecture, the normalisation statistics of the training split and an environment with torch. A separate pixi environment `ml` was added to the platform (python 3.12.14, pytorch-cpu 2.6.0, vbll 0.4.9 from PyPI), keeping the default environment torch-free. The reference data pipeline of the VBLL repository (`code/data.py`) was rerun on the x60 CSV to recover the normaliser: 253,361 events, of which 4,732 (1.87 percent) with reconstructed muon energy above 20 GeV were set aside as outliers, and the remaining 248,629 were shuffled with seed 42 and split 124,314 training against 124,315 validation events. The weights (22 tensors) were saved as numpy arrays next to a JSON with the architecture, the per-particle and per-component normaliser means and standard deviations, and provenance (checkpoint sha256 1bfae7df..., CSV fingerprint). A platform-side module rebuilt the network from that directory without importing the VBLL repository and loaded the weights with strict key checking. On the validation and outlier sets the port reproduced the reference implementation's predictive mean, sigma and class probabilities with a maximum absolute difference of 0.0 at the same torch seed. The README's evaluation numbers were found to be stochastic, because the heteroscedastic head of vbll 0.4.9 draws sigma from the posterior of its noise map on every call; over 100 seeds the muon energy sigma coefficient of variation (CV) was 0.3357 with standard deviation 0.0017 against the README's 0.3371, the proton's 0.4265 with 0.0027 against 0.4241, while the 68 percent coverages (0.8773 and 0.8380) and the outlier sigma ratios (2.8046 and 1.0828) matched the README's 0.877, 0.838, 2.80 and 1.08 to the printed precision. In physical units the model's muon energy sigma has a median of 1346 MeV on the validation set while the core width of its residual, 1.4826 times the median absolute deviation, is 477 MeV, with a pull width of 0.97: the Gaussian sigma is calibrated to the root-mean-square of a heavy-tailed residual, not to its core. For the proton the two numbers are 79 MeV and 61 MeV.

### Details

The network is an embedding of the particle type (2 x 8) concatenated with the normalised true 4-vector (E, px, py, pz), a multilayer perceptron 12 -> 64 -> 64 -> 64 with ReLU activations, and one `vbll.HetRegression` head per particle (64 -> 4, diagonal parameterisation, prior scale 1.0, noise prior scale 0.01), plus a `vbll.DiscClassification` head that is loaded but unused. Inputs are normalised per particle and component as x_norm = (x - mu) / (s + 1e-8) with mu and s the training-split mean and standard deviation of the true 4-vector in MeV; outputs are de-normalised with the reconstructed 4-vector's statistics. The predictive distribution of a head for hidden features h is Normal(W_mean h, sqrt((Var[W h] + 1) exp(s))) with s drawn from Normal(M_mean h, sqrt(exp(M_logdiag) h^2)), W and M the posteriors of the last layer and of the noise map. The 100-seed scan computed CV = std(sigma_E) / mean(sigma_E) over validation events and the outlier ratio median(sigma_E, outliers) / median(sigma_E, validation) with `evaluate.py`'s formulas. Code: `ndp/surrogate/vbll_model.py:VBLLSpec`, `build_module`, `VBLLSurrogateModel.from_dir`, `predict`, `smear`; `runs/2026-09-22_vbll_surrogate_1mu1p/step1_port.py`. Outputs: `surrogates/minerva_me_ccqelike_1mu1p/_vbll/x60_het/{arrays.npz, surrogate.json, checkpoint_best_model_x60_het.pt}`, `bench/step1_port.json`, `bench/step1_seed_scan.json`.

## Step 2: Provenance of the training pairs

The CSV that trained the checkpoint documents neither the frame of its 4-vectors, nor the selection its events passed, nor which true proton was paired with the reconstructed candidate, and the surrogate must be applied in its training frame to events defined the platform's way. The CSV's event identifiers (run, subrun and gate digits) identified it as the selected sample of all 61 StandardMC files of Playlist 1D, which the platform holds as row-aligned reconstruction and truth tables (11,360,928 rows). Every one of the 253,361 CSV events was matched to a distinct row on the true muon energy and x momentum rounded to 0.01 MeV, with 16 ties resolved by the muon momentum magnitude; an independent match on the reconstructed muon gave the same rows for all events. The true and reconstructed muon components and the reconstructed proton components were bit-identical to the cache's detector-frame columns in 100 percent of events and differed from their beam-rotated values by 0.31 GeV in median, so the CSV is detector-frame throughout, in MeV, with the reconstructed muon being `MasterAnaDev_leptonE` and the proton the `_fromdEdx` branches. The CSV's true proton equalled the platform's leading proton inside the signal window in 181,313 of the 181,344 rows that have one, was non-zero in all 72,017 rows with no proton in the window and in all 16,443 rows whose candidate's truth match is not a proton, so it is the leading true proton without the window, not the candidate's truth match. Against the platform's twelve-cut selection `minerva_ccqelike_1mu1p_v0`, 66,725 CSV events pass; the first failing cuts are the tracker z range (77,139), the apothem (35,541), the proton score (42,403), the Michel veto (8,744), the MINOS match (7,070), the muon window (5,403), the neutrino charge (4,925), the isolated-blob count (3,219), the proton window (1,450) and dead time (742). Conversely the platform selects 240,201 rows in 1D of which the CSV holds 27.8 percent. Among platform-selected rows, those in the CSV contain a true meson in 4.5 percent of cases against 57.2 percent for those the CSV lacks, have one hadron track and two prongs at the 95th percentile against two and three, and a candidate truth-matched to a proton in 99.2 against 89.9 percent; the CSV's reconstructed vertices span 4.0 to 8.6 m in z, so 30 percent lie upstream of the tracker in the nuclear-target region. The training sample is therefore a whole-detector, single-hadron-track, truth-meson-poor selection (62 percent quasi-elastic by GENIE interaction type). Its channel-signal fraction is 0.556 overall and 0.577 inside the tracker fiducial volume (0.320 signal-and-in-fiducial, since only 55 percent of its events are in the fiducial), against a purity of 0.49 for the platform's selection. The frame was written into the ported model's specification and the population mismatch was recorded as an open question for the physicist.

### Details

The match key was round(E x 1e5) << 26 + round(px x 1e5) + 2^25 on values in GeV, frame-invariant because the NuMI rotation is about x; the frame test compared, per event, the maximum absolute component difference with a tolerance of 1e-6 GeV against the cache's detector-frame vectors and against those vectors rotated by -0.05887 rad about x (`ndp.channels.observables.rotate_about_x`, `frame_rotation_angle`). The cutflow was evaluated with `ndp/adapters/minerva_anatuple.py:ccqelike_1mu1p_cutflow` and the channel's `selection.params`; the signal flag is the skim's `signal_minerva_ccqelike_1mu1p` column (beam frame, signal window) and the fiducial is the channel's `in_phase_space`. Code: `runs/2026-09-22_vbll_surrogate_1mu1p/step2_provenance.py:match`, `frame_test`. Outputs: `bench/step2_training_pairs.json`, `bench/step2_selection_scan.json`; `surrogate.json` (`spec.frame = "detector"`, `meta.training_pairs`); `docs/open_questions.md` entry of 2026-09-22.

## Step 3: The `vbll_event` surrogate kind

The platform's surrogates fold true-cell populations or a measurement's pair of truth observables, whereas the VBLL model needs whole 4-vectors and produces smeared 4-vectors from which any grid's reconstructed observable follows. A new surrogate kind was implemented that wraps a grid's binned response: for every signal event in the truth phase space it takes the primary muon and the leading proton inside the signal window (chosen in the channel frame, then rotated into the model's detector frame and converted to MeV), draws K = 20 reconstructed copies per particle from the model's predictive distribution, rebuilds the cached reconstruction columns from the smeared momenta (energies from the momentum and the particle mass, angles with respect to the beam, detector-frame components), evaluates the grid's reconstructed observable with the same functions the data go through, and histograms the copies with the event's weight times the efficiency of its true cell taken from the binned response. Because that efficiency already contains the loss from the selection's kinematic windows, the copies are conditioned on passing them: the event's weight is shared among its passing copies and an event with no passing copy is dropped and counted. Background and MC-statistical variance are delegated to the binned response, so only the migration changes. One smearing serves every grid. The kind was registered in the surrogate loader and exposed as `ndp run --surrogate-kind vbll` and `ndp surrogate build --kind vbll`. Four tests pass in the `ml` environment (a randomly initialised model of the same architecture: the smearing reproduces the model's own predictive to within statistics, the fold agrees with a binned response built from the model's own pairs, all 18 grids evaluate without NaN, the wrapper persists and reloads); the existing surrogate and measurement tests still pass. As a smoke test the wrapper for the muon momentum grid was built and its closure run over the 2,734,227 fiducial signal events of the official MC in 260 s: the folded total was 891,174 against 891,177 selected in-grid signal events, with 11 events dropped for having no passing copy, and 82.3 percent of copies passed the windows. On the GiBUU sample (56,132 signal events, 5 copies) 85.7 percent of copies passed the windows and all 18 grids evaluated without NaN; copies that pass the windows but fall outside a proton grid amount to 11.2 percent for the proton momentum grid and 16.9 percent for the proton momentum versus angle map, because those grids end at 1.1 GeV/c and 70 degrees while the reconstruction window extends to 1.3 GeV/c and 90 degrees.

### Details

For a measurement with reconstructed-cell index i and true-cell index j(e) of event e, the folded signal is N_i = sum_e w_e eps_j(e) (1 / n_pass(e)) sum_{k passing} 1[cell(reco_k(e)) = i], with eps the binned response's efficiency per true cell, n_pass the number of the K copies inside the muon window (theta < 17 degrees, 2 < p < 20 GeV/c) and proton window (theta < 90 degrees, 0.4 < p < 1.3 GeV/c) of `selection.params`, and events whose true cell lies outside the grid contribute nothing. The prediction compared with data is N_i plus the binned response's background at the data exposure. Synthetic columns: p from the smeared 3-momentum, E_mu = sqrt(p^2 + m_mu^2) and E_p = sqrt(p^2 + m_p^2) with m_mu = 0.1056583755 GeV and m_p = 0.93827208816 GeV, theta = arccos(p_z,beam / p) after rotating the detector-frame momentum by -0.05887 rad about x. The closure target is the column sum of the binned response's migration matrix, the selected signal with true cell inside the grid per reconstructed cell. Code: `ndp/surrogate/vbll.py:VBLLEventSurrogate`, `fold_tables`, `truth_4vectors`, `synthetic_reco`, `reco_window_pass`, `build_vbll`; `ndp/compare/folded.py:compare_folded` (branch `folding == "full"` with `fold_table`); `ndp/pipeline.py:wrap_surrogate`; `ndp/cli.py:_add_vbll_args`; `tests/test_surrogate_vbll.py`; `runs/2026-09-22_vbll_surrogate_1mu1p/step3_smoke.py`. Outputs: `surrogates/minerva_me_ccqelike_1mu1p/muon_p/vbll_x60_het/{arrays.npz, surrogate.json, closure.json}`, `bench/step3_smoke.json`; design recorded in `docs/decisions.md` and `docs/architecture.md`.

## Step 4: Muon momentum

The reconstructed muon momentum grid (8 bins from 2 to 20 GeV/c) was the first grid judged, chosen because the model's root-mean-square-calibrated energy sigma of 1.35 GeV against a 0.48 GeV core should show there. Two comparisons were made. First, the closure of Step 3 was drawn next to the exact binned fold: the ratio of the VBLL fold to the selected signal per bin is 0.968, 0.970, 0.965, 0.923, 1.014, 1.277, 1.141 and 0.702 for the bins 2 to 3, 3 to 4, 4 to 5, 5 to 6, 6 to 7.5, 7.5 to 10, 10 to 14 and 14 to 20 GeV/c, with the total ratio 1.0000; four of eight bins lie within the plan's proposed, not yet ratified, tolerance of 5 percent. Second, the GiBUU sample was folded with the new kind in a manifest-backed run and compared with the data on the same grid, next to the binned fold of the 2026-09-16 reference run, whose data and background cells were verified identical. Both migrations predict 377,228 events (175,118 signal, 202,110 background) against 329,653 data, a data-to-prediction ratio of 0.8739, since efficiency and background are shared and the window conditioning conserves the signal total (no GiBUU event lost). The Poisson -2lnL over 8 bins is 8052.3 with the VBLL migration and 7676.8 with the binned one (Pearson chi-square with MC statistics 7169.8 and 6806.2). Per bin the data-to-prediction ratio moves from 0.913 to 0.794 at 7.5 to 10 GeV/c and from 1.21 to 1.37 at 14 to 20 GeV/c while the four lowest bins change by 4 percent or less. The spread of the VBLL fold over ten independent seeds is 0.15 to 0.7 percent of the signal per bin, so the shape differences are properties of the model, not of the sampling. The efficiency source, the plan's open choice 3, was used as the proposed default (the efficiency of the true cell of the grid's own binned response) and recorded as in use but not ratified. The judgment of the closure is the physicist's and had not been given when this was written.

### Details

-2lnL = 2 sum_i [m_i - d_i + d_i ln(d_i / m_i)] over bins with prediction m_i > 0 and data d_i (Baker-Cousins), the Pearson term sum_i (d_i - m_i)^2 / (m_i + var_MC,i) with var_MC,i the binned response's MC-statistical variance of the fold (`ndp/compare/folded.py:poisson_gof`). The normalisation of the GiBUU truth to the data exposure is N_true = sigma_cell x N_nucleons(3.23e30) x Phi(6.32e-8 cm^-2 per POT) x POT(1.0574e21), i.e. a weight scale of 2,158,444.6 per unit of GiBUU weight (`compare_folded`'s `expected`). The band was the standard deviation over seeds 1 to 10 of `VBLLEventSurrogate.fold_table` applied to the GiBUU truth with those weights; the seed-0 fold reproduced the run's signal cells. Code: `runs/2026-09-22_vbll_surrogate_1mu1p/step4_muon_p.py`; `ndp/pipeline.py:run_model_multi` with `surrogate_kind="vbll"`. Figures: `figs/vbll_surrogate_closure_muon_p.png` (selected signal of the official MC with the exact binned fold and the VBLL fold, ratio panel with the proposed 5 percent band), `figs/vbll_surrogate_folded_muon_p.png` (data against both predictions with the shared background and the seed band, prediction-to-data panel); both carry the preliminary watermark because the folded mode has no systematic uncertainties. Outputs: `bench/step4_muon_p.json`; run `runs/2026-09-22_gibuu_2025_me_fhc_c12__minerva_me_ccqelike_1mu1p__muon_p__vbll/`.

## Report figure: GiBUU muon kinematics at three stages

To show what the signal definition and the surrogate do to the generator's muons, the muon momentum and the angle with respect to the beam were histogrammed for the full GiBUU charged-current sample without any cut, for the events passing the channel's truth signal definition, and for the signal events after the VBLL smearing, the last both without and with the per-true-cell efficiency of the muon momentum and muon angle binned responses. Weights are the GiBUU event weights scaled to the data exposure, so the histograms count events in the fiducial volume at 1.0574e21 POT. The truth signal cutflow is 979,395 generated numu CC events (8,902,466 at the data exposure), 578,821 after the muon window (5,172,886), 207,144 with a proton in the window (1,860,686), 56,155 after the meson veto (533,253), 56,132 after the heavy-baryon veto (533,040) and unchanged by the photon veto; the sample carries no detector geometry so the fiducial requirement is not applied. The smearing conserves the signal total (533,040 at the data exposure; 85.6 percent of copies pass the reconstruction windows) and the efficiency reduces it to 175,118 on the momentum grid and 174,171 on the angle grid. In the 2 to 20 GeV/c window the median true signal momentum is 5.25 GeV/c and remains 5.25 GeV/c after smearing, with the peak bin moving from 5.0 to 5.5 down to 4.5 to 5.0 GeV/c and the tail above 10 GeV/c lowered; in angle the median moves from 7.25 degrees at truth to 6.75 degrees after smearing and 6.25 degrees after the efficiency weighting, the peak bin from 6.0 to 6.5 to 5.0 to 5.5 degrees.

### Details

Observables: p = |p_mu| and theta = arccos(p_z / p) in the beam frame from the truth table's `lep_px, lep_py, lep_pz` (GiBUU tables are beam-native), via `ndp.channels.observables.lep_p` and `lep_theta_deg`; the cutflow is `ChannelSpec.signal_cutflow`, the signal definition of arXiv:2503.15047 as the channel manifest states it. The smeared distributions use the same K = 20 copies, window conditioning and efficiency lookup as the surrogate kind (`ndp/surrogate/vbll.py:synthetic_reco`, `reco_window_pass`), drawn with seed 0. Code: `runs/2026-09-22_vbll_surrogate_1mu1p/report_gibuu_muon_distributions.py`. Figure: `figs/vbll_surrogate_gibuu_muon_p_theta.png`, four panels: momentum 0 to 30 GeV/c and angle 0 to 90 degrees on logarithmic axes, and the signal windows 2 to 20 GeV/c and 0 to 17 degrees on linear axes, five curves each (charged-current truth, truth signal, truth signal times efficiency, smeared signal, smeared signal times efficiency). No uncertainties are drawn. Histogram contents: `bench/report_gibuu_muon_distributions.json`.
